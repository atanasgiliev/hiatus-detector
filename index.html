Greek Hiatus Detector

An analytical tool for identifying vowel hiatus in Ancient Greek texts

Help & Usage

How to use the detector

  1. Upload or paste a Greek text file.
  2. Choose whether you want normal detection or “same sound only”.
  3. Run the detector.
  4. Results will appear below with line numbers and statistics.

What counts as a hiatus?

A hiatus occurs when one vowel is immediately followed by another vowel and they do not form a diphthong.

Examples of hiatus

  • α ε
  • ο α
  • η ω

What does NOT count as hiatus?

Regular diphthongs

αι, ει, οι, αυ, ευ, ου, ηυ, ωυ

Diphthongs with iota subscript / adscript

ᾳ, ῃ, ῳ (and equivalent forms)

Broken diphthongs (these DO count as hiatus)

  • αϊ (diaeresis)
  • if the accent is on the first vowel of the pair

“Same sound only” option

When enabled, the detector reports only hiatuses where both vowels have the same base sound.

Examples detected

  • α α
  • ο ο
  • οι οι

Examples ignored

  • α ε
  • ο αι

Accents and breathings are ignored when comparing sounds.

Hiatus Types to Detect

At least one hiatus type must be selected.

Optional Hiatus Rules

These options are disabled by default.

Output Options

Instances of Across-Verse (V) type hiatus count towards both of the verses they span.

Highlight Legend

  • Red — Word-internal hiatus (I)
  • Green — Inter-word hiatus (B)
  • Blue — Across-verse hiatus (V)
